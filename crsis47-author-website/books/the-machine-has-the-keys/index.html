T+47 / 03 — Act

Book 3 · Act

The Machine Has the Keys

By Constantin Jean-Pierre

When intelligent systems hold the keys, who remains accountable for the doors they open?

Synopsis

The machine is ready to move. Who gave it permission—and who will answer for what happens next?

The Machine Has the Keys begins where a convincing assessment becomes consequential power. A system that can identify danger may also be allowed to interrupt work, restrict access, or change another person’s choices. Being capable of an action does not settle whether it is authorized. Being right does not remove the need for brakes.

Through personal reflection and illustrative security-operations scenarios, Constantin Jean-Pierre puts responsibility beside capability. Who can stop the system? Which permissions should remain out of reach? Can an action be reversed? What happens to the person on the other side when the machine gets it wrong—and who has the authority to take the keys back?

At ACT, the third stage of T+47, evidence and reasoning become questions of control, reversibility, and accountability. These scenarios are thought experiments, not demonstrations of autonomous CRSIS-47 capability. Their pressure falls on a human decision: what may we delegate without surrendering the responsibility to govern it?
