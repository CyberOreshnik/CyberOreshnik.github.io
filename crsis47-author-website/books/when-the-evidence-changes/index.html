T+47 / 02 — Reason

Book Two · Reason

When the Evidence Changes

By Constantin Jean-Pierre

What happens when new evidence challenges the conclusion everyone believed was correct?

Synopsis

The evidence looked convincing. Then something else arrived. The earlier observations were still true; the story they seemed to tell was no longer enough.

When the Evidence Changes takes readers into the difficult territory between suspicion and proof. Constantin Jean-Pierre follows the reasoning problem that emerges when an alarming sequence gains legitimate context—and asks whether people and machines can revise an assessment without defending their first answer at all costs.

The consequences do not stay on a screen. A legitimate person can become the target of a premature conclusion. A reassuring explanation can also be accepted too easily. Between those failures lies the work of showing what was observed, what was inferred, why confidence changed, and what remains unknown.

As REASON, the second stage of T+47, this book carries the opening question about knowing enough into a more demanding test: can an intelligent system change its mind for reasons we can inspect? Before granting the machine authority to act, Jean-Pierre asks readers to look closely at the judgment that would guide it—and the human responsibility that remains when that judgment changes.
