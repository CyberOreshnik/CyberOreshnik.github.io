Book 01Know

The 47-Second Question

Recognizing the decision problem.

What happens when the truth arrives one piece at a time—and a decision cannot wait?

By the time the damage is visible, the chance to intervene may already have passed. The 47-Second Question begins with that unsettling gap and follows Constantin Jean-Pierre toward a question that will not let him go: how soon could anyone have known? Evidence arrives in fragments. An alert can mislead. Waiting has a cost. Opening the T+47 series at KNOW, this book asks what it takes to recognize danger without turning urgency into an excuse for being wrong.

Book 02Reason

When the Evidence Changes

Revising judgments as evidence develops.

What happens when new evidence challenges the conclusion everyone believed was correct?

The alert was real. The conclusion may not be. When the Evidence Changes follows the uneasy moment when missing context unsettles an answer that once seemed obvious. What does a machine owe the person it has already treated as a threat? What does an analyst owe the evidence? At REASON, the second stage of T+47, Jean-Pierre confronts the pull of the first answer—and the courage, discipline, and accountability it takes to change it.

Book 03Act

The Machine Has the Keys

Examining delegated action and control.

When intelligent systems hold the keys, who remains accountable for the doors they open?

A warning asks for attention. An action changes someone’s world. The Machine Has the Keys crosses the boundary between recognizing danger and giving an intelligent system permission to respond. Through personal reflection and illustrative security scenarios, Jean-Pierre confronts the safeguards that matter most: who can stop the machine, reverse its decisions, or withdraw its authority? At ACT, the third stage of T+47, the keys become a responsibility that human beings cannot hand away with the task.

Explore Book 03 ↗
Book 04Confront

The Other Machine Has Keys Too

Considering adversarial and competing machine agency.

What happens when another machine possesses the authority to challenge the first?

Your machine has learned to respond. Another is learning how it responds. The Other Machine Has Keys Too moves beyond the authority of a single system into a contest of observation, adaptation, and competing decisions. In this blend of speculative storytelling and analysis, a defensive move becomes information for the other side. CONFRONT, the fourth stage of T+47, asks who remains accountable when two intelligent systems can each change the choices available to the other.
