CRSIS-47 | Cybersecurity Research & Innovation

Research & future vision / 01

The first warning
is rarely the whole truth.

How early can an intelligent cybersecurity system make a defensible decision when the evidence is still unfolding?

In cybersecurity, evidence rarely arrives as a complete picture. A suspicious login may indicate an intrusion—or legitimate activity. A privileged action may signal compromise—or an authorized change.

The cost of waiting can be severe.
So can the cost of accusing too soon.

CRSIS-47 investigates the earliest moment an intelligent system can make a defensible security decision as evidence progressively emerges.

Through controlled experiments, the research examines how detection timing, classification accuracy, uncertainty, and false-positive risk interact.

The objective is not simply to make artificial intelligence faster. It is to make its decisions measurable, accountable, and worthy of trust.

Illustrative assessment / T+0

UNCERTAIN

Limited evidence

Why this conclusion?

An unusual schedule is an observation. Unauthorized activity is only one possible explanation.

What remains uncertain?

Was this work approved? What is being exported, and where is it going?

Confidence must follow the evidence. These qualitative labels are explanations, not calibrated probabilities or performance scores.

Checkpoint 1 of 6
What do the three classifications mean?
UNCERTAIN
The evidence does not yet justify a sufficiently definite interpretation.
BENIGN
The available evidence supports a legitimate explanation, subject to revision.
MALICIOUS
The available evidence supports unauthorized activity. Attribution and impact may remain unresolved.

This example is transparent and bundled in the website. It is not a blinded experiment and uses none of the protected research scenarios.

What the example helps explain

Time to confident detection (TTCD)
The time at which an assessment first meets a study’s predeclared detection rule. A defensible measure needs a defined origin, threshold and clock; this teaching interface computes none.
Threshold crossings and false positives
A qualifying malicious assessment must satisfy the classification and probability rule together. A crossing in a benign reference case is a false-positive concern, even if a later answer is correct.
Misses, revisions and final accuracy
A case may end with the right classification yet miss a timing threshold. Revising an assessment as exculpatory or incriminating evidence arrives should remain visible in the record.
Calibration, uncertainty and oversight
Confidence is not proof. Probability calibration requires evaluation against reference outcomes across cases; human-entered estimates are not automatically calibrated. Human oversight and accountable reasoning remain essential.

The next frontier

Intelligence should be
demonstrated—not merely claimed.

Imagine independent AI security agents confronting the same unfamiliar cybersecurity scenario. Each receives only the evidence available at a given moment. Each must decide what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and whether a confident intrusion classification is justified.

Their performance is evaluated against protected ground truth—not marketing claims, model reputation, or speed alone.

This is the longer-term vision behind the proposed CRSIS-47 Bittensor research extension: a decentralized environment in which competing detection approaches could be assessed for accuracy, timeliness, calibration, and disciplined use of evidence.

The foundation must be scientifically credible before the competition can be meaningful.

The research principle

Speed without evidence
is not intelligence.
Confidence without accountability
is not trust.

CRSIS-47 is being developed to investigate whether intelligent cybersecurity systems can recognize threats earlier while remaining disciplined about what the evidence does—and does not—justify.

The long-term ambition is a reproducible evaluation framework in which competing AI systems must demonstrate their capabilities under controlled, independently reviewable conditions.
